# Frame

This file states the problem in one sentence and lists what the fields that study papers call its parts.

## The problem in one sentence

How should a research article be ordered so that a reader can find, judge and reuse its claims, and does the standard order do that?

## What the field calls it

| Name | Field | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **IMRaD** (also IMRAD, IMRD) | scientific writing, publication science | Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion as the sections of an article |
| **Research article (RA) genre** | genre analysis, English for Specific Purposes (ESP) | the article as a recurring social form with expected parts |
| **Move** and **step** | genre analysis | a stretch of text that does one rhetorical job, and its sub-jobs |
| **CARS** (Create a Research Space) | genre analysis | the three moves of an introduction (Swales 1990) |
| **Hourglass model** | genre analysis, writing teaching | general to specific in the introduction, specific to general in the discussion (Hill 1982) |
| **Metadiscourse** | applied linguistics | text about the text: signposts, hedges, boosters, self-mention (Hyland 2004) |
| **Structured abstract** (also "more informative abstract") | medical editing, information science | an abstract with fixed labelled parts (Ad Hoc Working Group 1987) |
| **Graphical abstract**, **visual abstract** | publishing | one image that summarises the article |
| **Highlights** | publishing | three to five short bullet points with the main findings (Elsevier highlights) |
| **Reporting guideline** | research methodology | a checklist of what a report of one study design must contain (EQUATOR) |
| **Completeness of reporting** | meta-research | the share of checklist items a report actually contains |
| **Scholarly reading patterns**, **information-seeking behaviour** | library and information science | how many articles researchers read, for how long, and which parts |
| **Strategic reading** | information science | working across many articles at once, by fragments (Renear 2009) |
| **Registered Report** (RR), **results-free review** | meta-science | peer review of question and method before the results exist |
| **HARKing** | meta-science | presenting a post hoc hypothesis as if it were a priori (Kerr 1998) |
| **Reproducible research**, **executable paper**, **literate programming** | computational science | the article shipped with the code and data that produce it |
| **Nanopublication**, **modular publishing** | semantic publishing | the article split into machine-readable claims, or into one unit per research stage |
| **Readability formula** (Flesch Reading Ease, FRE; New Dale-Chall, NDC) | psycholinguistics, bibliometrics | a score from sentence length, word length or word familiarity |
| **Document class**, **article type** | typesetting, publishing (JATS) | the template a publisher supplies, and the label a journal gives a manuscript |

## Out of scope

- Mathematics, theoretical computer science and the humanities depart from IMRaD; a companion review in this repository covers mathematical articles with proofs.
- Peer review as a process, citation practice and authorship rules are left out.
