# State of the art

This file says who holds the record on each sub-question: the most adopted practice, kept apart from the best evidence.

## Most adopted (practice record)

| Sub-question | Holder | What it is | Evidence of [adoption](../academic-papers/state-of-the-art.md) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Report components | NISO 2010 | required title section, abstract, contents, summary, introduction, methods, results and discussion, conclusions | a US standard reaffirmed in 2010; a US Air Force laboratory guide uses it (not verified: no guide was reached; search summary only) |
| Thesis form | the simple (introduction, literature, method, results, discussion, conclusion) plan | the plan taught by university handouts | handouts only; no count found |
| Summary first | NISO 2010 and engineering firms | a non-technical executive summary, often prepared for a long report | Sheffield 2017 interviews; no adoption count |
| French internship report | school guides such as EOST 2026 and TSE 2017 | company, missions, technical work, conclusion with distance; defence before a jury | two guides; no count found |
| French grading | a grid with form and substance | TSE 2017 (7 and 13 of 20), INSPE 2021 (15 written of 20) | two grids; no count found |

## Best evidence (research record)

| Sub-question | Holder | Method | Measured how |
|---|---|---|---|
| Genres of student writing | Gardner 2013, Nesi 2012 | corpus classification of 2,858 texts | counts by family (Table 4) and by level and disciplinary group (tables under Figures 1 and 2) |
| Which parts engineers read | Pinelli 1982 | survey, 378 internal and 504 external respondents | self-reported share reading each component, and ranked order |
| How examiners judge | Mullins 2002 | 30 interviews | themes (full text), no counts of criteria |
| Headings and recall | Lorch 1996 | two experiments (free recall, N = 80; summarization, N = 99) | free recall and summaries |
| Structured abstract | Hartley 2004 | narrative review of 31 studies | information content, readability |

## Records kept apart

- Highest self-reported rate: in a survey of 882 readers the conclusion is read by 97% (Pinelli 1982, measured as a self-report survey, 1982, one agency).
- Not established: any effect of an executive summary, conclusion-first order or length on report readers' accuracy or time.
- A lower bound would be an impossibility result; none applies here.
