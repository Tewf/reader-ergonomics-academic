# Review

Surveys first, then the seminal work, then the work that cites it.
Grades are defined in README.md; the evidence against is gathered in against.md.

## 1. History and prevalence of IMRaD

| Work | Kind | Finding | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross 2002 | survey (history) | the scientific article in English, French and German from the 17th century to the present | supported with caveat |
| Atkinson 1999 | survey (corpus history) | Philosophical Transactions 1675-1975: rhetoric moves from author-centred to object-centred | supported with caveat |
| Bazerman 1988 | survey (rhetoric) | the experimental report took its form in answer to rhetorical and social situations | supported with caveat |
| Sollaci 2004 | seminal (counts) | 1,297 articles in BMJ, JAMA, Lancet and NEJM, 1935-1985: IMRaD above 10% by 1950, above 80% in the 1970s, the only pattern in the 1980s | measured |
| Gastel 2016 | handbook (Day's) | teaches IMRaD as the default shape of a primary research paper | convention, untested |
| ICMJE | editorial standard | original research usually divided into IMRAD; reviews, case reports and editorials may differ | convention, untested |
| Lin 2012 | forward (corpus) | 433 empirical articles, 39 disciplines: plain IMRD is not the default; ILM[RD]C ranks first | measured |
| Moskovitz 2024 | forward (corpus) | biology mostly IMRaD; under half in engineering and social science; most computational papers not IMRaD | measured |

## 4. Abstracts and front matter

| Work | Kind | Finding | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ad Hoc Working Group 1987 | seminal proposal | structured abstracts for clinical articles, with fixed labelled parts | convention, untested |
| Haynes 1990 | forward | revisits the proposal after journals adopted it, and refines the headings | supported with caveat |
| Hartley 2004a | survey (31 studies) | structured abstracts are longer (+21%; +35% when independently published), usually more informative, sometimes easier to read and search | measured |
| Hartley 2014 | survey update | spread beyond medicine; early studies biased by rewritten abstracts and student judges | supported with caveat |
| Taddio 1994 | before and after | 300 abstracts, 33 criteria: quality 0.57 unstructured vs 0.74 structured | measured |
| Sharma 2006 | controlled comparison | 600 dental abstracts, 29-item checklist: scores rose only in journals that switched | measured |
| Hartley 2003 | rewriting study | 24 psychology abstracts: longer, more informative, more readable, judged clearer by 48 authors | measured |
| Budgen 2008 | randomised paired judgement | software engineering, 64 judges, 25 papers: completeness +6.65 on an 18-item questionnaire, clarity +2.98 on 1-10; 40 of 57 preferred structured | measured |
| Pferschy-Wenzig 2016 | observational | one journal, 2014-2015: papers without a graphical abstract had more downloads, views and citations | supported with caveat |
| Kim 2022 | observational | 4,205 gastroenterology articles: with a graphical abstract, median citations 14 vs 12 | supported with caveat |
| Ibrahim 2017 | crossover | 44 articles tweeted both ways: visual abstract 7.7 times the impressions, 2.7 times the visits (secondary) | supported with caveat |
| Oska 2020 | crossover, random order | 40 articles: visual abstract tweets drew about twice the views of citation or key-figure tweets | measured |
| Chapman 2019 | randomised trial | 41 manuscripts: visual beat plain-English abstracts on engagement; public engagement low throughout | measured |
| Elsevier highlights | publisher guideline | 3 to 5 bullets of at most 85 characters, meant for discoverability | convention, untested |

## 5. How researchers read

| Work | Kind | Finding | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tenopir 2008 | survey series since 1977 | readings per scientist nearly doubled 1977-2005; minutes per reading fell from 48 to 31 | supported with caveat |
| Tenopir 2015 | same series, 2012 wave | about 21 articles a month, about 32 minutes each | supported with caveat |
| Nicholas 2007 | log analysis | many users of journal sites view a few items and do not come back (bouncing) | supported with caveat |
| Renear 2009 | review | scientists read strategically: search, filter, scan, link and extract fragments | supported with caveat |
| Hubbard 2017 | survey, 260 undergraduates and researchers in biological sciences at one UK university | experienced readers rank figures and results first, undergraduates the abstract; all rank the introduction low | supported with caveat |
| Keshav 2007 | advice | three passes: 5-10 minutes on title, abstract, introduction, headings, conclusions; up to an hour with the figures; 4-5 hours for a novice to re-derive it | convention, untested |
| Mensh 2017 | advice | context, content, conclusion at the scale of paper, section and paragraph | convention, untested |

## 8. Templates in use

| Template | Owner | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| elsarticle | Elsevier, on CTAN | layout options (preprint, review, 1p, 3p, 5p), front matter (author, affiliation, email, abstract, graphical abstract, highlights, keywords), reference style |
| llncs | Springer, on CTAN | page layout, institute and keywords macros, theorem-like environments, the splncs04 reference style |
| IEEEtran | IEEE, on CTAN | journal and conference modes, IEEE keywords block, drop-cap first paragraph, IEEE reference style |
| acmart | ACM, on CTAN | formats (sigconf, acmsmall and others), CCS concepts, keywords, rights block, DOI, teaser figure, figure descriptions for accessibility |
| arXiv | preprint server | takes TeX source with no house template; HTML versions since 2023, because 2.4% of research outputs met accessibility guidelines (Frankston 2024) |
| JATS | NISO standard | machine-readable article metadata, including an article-type attribute |
| ICMJE and journals | article types | research article, review, case report, letter, each with its own word limits and abstract format |

All of them fix layout, metadata and reference style; none fixes the argument, which stays with the writer.
The IEEEtran journal sample holds an Introduction, a Conclusion and appendices; the elsarticle sample holds one example section.
